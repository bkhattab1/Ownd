## TL;DR: what OWND should copy

1. **One hero product, numbered drops.** Wants and Needs sells essentially one thing (a heavyweight sweatsuit) and releases it as `001`, `002`, `003`, `004`. OWND's "Chapter 01, 02…" is the same mechanic with a story on top. Start Chapter 01 with **one hero piece** (hoodie or full sweatsuit), not a range.
2. **Waitlist before every drop.** W&N runs "coming soon, join the waitlist" posts, then "available now", then "almost out of stock, join the email list for 003". The loop is: tease → waitlist → live → scarcity → next number. Cheap, and it doubles as pre-order validation for a 5,000 MAD budget.
3. **Skits, not lookbooks.** W&N's TikTok is relatable comedy skits and challenges with the product in frame ("clothing brand mishap", reaction videos, "how many could you get?"). Broken Planet also grew mainly through TikTok. This costs 0 MAD and only needs the two cousins and a phone.
4. **Price in the local mid band.** Moroccan streetwear today sits at **~170 MAD** (entry tee) to **~600 MAD** (hoodie), with some pieces near 1,000 MAD. OWND's Blank Page essentials should sit below Streetan; Chapter pieces can sit at or slightly above it because they are numbered and storied.
5. **A message bigger than the clothes.** Every small brand that broke out sells a value: W&N "Do what you need to get what you want", Trikko "effort, passion, sacrifice", Drop "Made for those who never had sh*t". OWND's "Own your story" fits this exactly, and the founder story (14 → 19, "not real work") is stronger than most of these.

## 1. Wants and Needs (@wantsandneedsbrand), the marketing reference

US brand, sold at [wantsandneeds.com](https://wantsandneeds.com/). Tagline: *"Take action. Do what you need to get what you want."* Site copy: *"Everyday pieces that just work. Clean design, comfortable fits, and quality you can feel."*

**Product range (drop 004, live now):** the range grew outward from the hero sweatpants.

| Piece | Price (USD) | ≈ MAD |
|---|---|---|
| Baggy sweatpants (brown / navy) | $69.99 | ~665 |
| Hoodie (brown / navy / black) | $69.99 | ~665 |
| Heather grey zip-up / sweatpants | $70 | ~665 |
| Baggy tee (2 colours) | $40 | ~380 |
| Sweat shorts (2 colours) | $45 | ~430 |
| Jorts / jeans | $50–79.99 | ~475–760 |
| Sherpa jacket | $87.99 (was $109.99) | ~835 |

Several colourways were already **sold out** (navy hoodie, black hoodie, navy sweatpants, black jeans), which the site leaves visible.

**Drop model**
- Numbered drops: `001` → `002 Sweatsuit` → `003` → `004 IS LIVE`. Each number is a moment.
- Pre-drop: "002 coming soon! Join the waitlist to stay notified, link in bio."
- Live: "002 Sweatsuit is available now! Link in bio."
- Late stage: "Sweatsuits almost out of stock! Join the email list for updates on 003" and "Get 10% off when joining the email list."
- Hero piece first (sweatsuit), then adds tees, shorts, denim and outerwear around it.
- Shipping: free US shipping over $100 (a soft bundle nudge: hoodie + sweatpants clears it). Ships within 5 business days, 14-day returns.

**Content patterns** (from TikTok and YouTube Shorts listings)
- Short comedy skits and relatable situations with the sweatsuit worn, not a product shot ("Baggy Sweats: A Clothing Brand Mishap", reaction videos, "Knew something was up").
- Challenges / games ("How many could you get?", "under the ball" challenge).
- Every caption ends with a call to action: waitlist, link in bio, email list, 10% off.
- Same hashtags each time: `#clothingbrand #streetwear #sweatsuit #skit`.
- Cross-posted to YouTube Shorts and Facebook.

## 3. UK benchmarks (how small brands became big)

- **Broken Planet** (London, 2020). Two founders started in lockdown with about **5 samples**, organic-cotton graphic hoodies, and grew almost entirely on TikTok. Runs a raffle ("Friends and Family", £3 a ticket) for **15-unit** pieces. ([source](https://hypebeast.com/2022/9/broken-planet-market-interview-feature-hypebeast-uk))
- **Corteiz** (London). Private Instagram, a password-protected site, drops that sell out in minutes, and no paid ads or influencers. Grew through street events like the "Da Great Bolo Exchange", where fans swapped designer jackets for unreleased pieces and the jackets went to charity. ([source](https://lovecreative.com/blog/3x-ways-corteiz-is-breaking-all-the-rules))
- **Represent** (Manchester, 2012). Started as a college project making graphic tees in a garden shed. The founder took no salary for 7–8 years, and "people around him thought his business was a joke." That last line is OWND's own story. ([source](https://www.recall.it/summary/entertainment/from-my-garden-shed-to-dollar100m-business-empire-that-letter-was-the-end-of-represent-george-heaton))

## 4. Patterns, and what they mean for OWND

| Pattern seen | Who | OWND move |
|---|---|---|
| Hero product before a range | W&N (sweatsuit), Broken Planet (hoodie), Drop (tee) | Chapter 01 = one hero piece in 1–2 colours. Add the matching sweatpants in Chapter 02 so the sweatsuit becomes the signature set. |
| Numbered, timed drops | W&N 001–004, Drop, 99 By JMS (100 pcs) | "Chapter 01 / Page 001 of 050". Print the unit number inside the garment; it doubles as the story's page number. |
| Waitlist → live → almost gone → next number | W&N | WhatsApp/Instagram broadcast list + a simple form. Open pre-orders only to the waitlist first, which funds production. |
| Tiny raffle/numbered "grail" pieces | Broken Planet (15 pcs) | A 10-piece "signed by the founders" variant per Chapter. |
| Entry product to lower the first purchase | Drop (170 MAD tee), W&N ($40 tee) | Blank Page tee around 199–249 MAD; Chapter hoodie around 449–549 MAD (below Streetan's 579–599 until the brand has proof). |
| Value bigger than the clothes | W&N, Trikko, Drop | "Own your story" + the 14 → 19 founder manifesto as content pillar #1. |
| Lo-fi, founder-on-camera content | W&N skits, Broken Planet TikTok | Weekly skits on "online business isn't real work" situations, family reactions, cousin banter. 0 MAD. |
| Local places as backdrops | Gens du Monde, 99 By JMS | Shoot in the founders' own city and recognisable streets, not a studio. |
| Community events over ads | Corteiz, Atelier Rouge | Later: a "write your chapter" story swap or meet-up per drop. Keep ad spend near zero until Chapter 02. |
